# The multilingual guide against the product — gap analysis

Tor, 2026-09-27, uploaded «Orgpuls Multilingual Implementation Guide: Platform (nb/en) and Survey Languages for Norway». Every recommendation in it was mapped against the code as it stands (main at a91e2bd), by five mappers, one per area, each checked item by item by a second agent; 22 statuses were corrected in that check. Nothing here is built yet except where it says so. X-067 records it.

## In one paragraph

The guide's Phase 1 is where the product is: bokmål and English for the platform and the survey, English offered to a respondent only when every item and the survey pages' strings are approved (lib/i18n/offered.ts), and language kept out of answers and reports. What it asks next splits three ways. **Engineering foundations** the later languages need (CI gates for ICU, CLDR plurals and SMS encoding; one locale registry; a translation workflow with status, notes, immutability and per-round pinning; per-organisation language pilots) can be built now without anyone else. **Methodology and people** — two professional translators per language, a methodologist adjudicating, cognitive interviews, licensing from STAMI or the Nordic Council of Ministers — must come before any Polish, Ukrainian or Lithuanian item goes live, and before English can be called a validated version. **A handful of recommendations contradict CLAUDE.md or a logged decision**; those are listed below with what the product does instead, and the ones that are genuinely open are Tor's.

## Found and fixed while mapping

- **An organisation's own invitation SMS went to every recipient, whatever their language.** `smsLead` (supabase/functions/_shared/mail.ts) now uses the organisation's text only for recipients who get the invitation in the organisation's language; everyone else gets the approved default in their own. No hosted organisation had a custom SMS text or SMS turned on, so nobody received a mixed message. Deployed (dispatcher v31), with a unit test.

## Decisions that are Tor's

1. **English is a reviewed machine translation, not a validated adaptation.** The guide never lets machine or LLM text be the final wording of a validated item. D-127 and X-065 chose otherwise: the English was written with AI assistance, and Tor's approval (2026-09-27, 10:55, 131 items and the survey pages' strings) is the human review. The QPS Nordic instrument has an official English version (TemaNord 2000:603). Options: (a) a methodologist compares each English core statement with the official QPS Nordic English and the rows are re-seeded as `official` where they correspond; (b) keep English as it is and say what it is — a reviewed translation — wherever results in English are compared with bokmål. The Likert anchors belong to the same question.
2. **Licensing before any new language.** Written confirmation from STAMI and/or the Nordic Council of Ministers before publishing Polish, Ukrainian or Lithuanian versions under the QPS Nordic name; if limited, the guide's open decision to call them «Orgpuls core (based on QPS Nordic)».
3. **Which languages, when, and who translates.** The guide: pl and uk in parallel, then lt; sv/da cheaply from the official Nordic versions; nn as a sales-led decision for municipalities, schools and kindergartens; ar/so/ti on demand. Each needs a TRAPD vendor (two translators), an adjudicator and 5–8 cognitive interviews through pilot customers.
4. **The SMS budget for UCS-2 languages.** Polish, Lithuanian and Ukrainian force UCS-2 (70 characters, 67 per segment). The guide budgets two segments (≤134 characters with the link); engagement-phases.md § 2.3 allowed three.
5. **The respondent screens' type scale.** The guide wants ≥16 px text and 1.5 line-height; the respondent flow follows the design bundle (14 px root, options at 14.5 px). Raising it is a deviation from the design and new baselines.
6. **A welcome screen with a bilingual language label.** The flow starts on question 1 (D-127); the guide wants the picker repeated under a welcome text labelled «Språk / Language». The design has no such screen.
7. **The browser's language and remembering the choice.** D-96 deliberately ignores Accept-Language, and D-127 keeps the choice in the address only, never a cookie. The guide would use both. Keeping the decisions is consistent; changing them is a D-96/D-127 amendment.
8. **Formal reports in bokmål by default.** The guide: the Arbeidstilsynet-facing report is always bokmål, with «also in English». Today it renders in the reader's interface language.
9. **A DPIA** that records language as a quasi-identifier, and any invariance-testing pipeline (the guide's multi-group CFA/DIF at n ≥ 200 per language) — which must never touch the production answer tables — waits on it.
10. **Accessibility.** A certified WCAG 2.1 AA audit of the respondent flow first (municipal customers are bound by 2.1), then a voluntary accessibility statement, which would go through the legal review.

## Where the guide and the product's rules differ, and what the product does

These are not open: CLAUDE.md or a logged decision settles them, and the guide's intent is met another way.

- **Let next-intl middleware handle locale negotiation, redirects/rewrites and Link hreflang headers (localeDetection/alternateLinks config)** (§4 Library choice (routing/middleware)). CLAUDE.md Stack: 'i18n: next-intl, no locale routing (no /no or /en prefix)… the locale comes from the user's profile, the organisation default, or a cookie'. middleware.ts:10-12 only calls safeUpdateSession, with no next-intl createMiddleware. Locale is resolved in lib/i18n/request.ts:29-32: en.orgpuls.com gives en, otherwise the NEXT_LOCALE cookie, otherwise 'no'. D-96 (docs/DEVIATIONS.md:4058-4062) deliberately does not use browser language, and D-98 (DEVIATIONS.md:4204-4205) makes the host decide. → *Do not adopt next-intl routing middleware. Keep host plus cookie resolution. hreflang is emitted per page by lib/marketing/meta.ts instead (see §8 items).*
- **Use BCP 47 'nb' (not 'no') as the Bokmål locale code** (§4 Locale codes). CLAUDE.md: '`no` is the source language'. The internal code is 'no' in lib/i18n/locales.ts:6,9-10 and messages/no.json, and in DB check constraints: supabase/migrations/0001_foundation.sql:45,53 (default_lang, profiles.lang), 0055_crm.sql:82,122, 0079_item_translations.sql:35 (employees.language), plus lib/i18n/respondent-ui.json keys. Output surfaces already map to nb: app/layout.tsx:36-38 (html lang no→nb), components/i18n/LanguageSwitch.tsx:59-60, lib/marketing/meta.ts:37 (hreflang nb), lib/marketing/schema.ts:32 (nb-NO). Two places leak the raw code: app/s/[token]/page.tsx:90 `<main lang={lang}>` emits lang="no", and components/respond/RespondFlow.tsx:387-388 has hrefLang/lang={o.code}. → *Keep 'no' as the internal code. Renaming would touch at least 6 migrations' check constraints, the message files, hashes and flags, and CLAUDE.md fixes the code. Add a bcp47(locale) helper (no→nb) in lib/i18n/locales.ts and use it for every lang, hrefLang and inLanguage output, which also fixes the two leaks.*
- **Load script fallback fonts via next/font with subsets (e.g. subsets: ['cyrillic'])** (§4 Fonts). CLAUDE.md: 'Fonts are served from public/fonts by app/fonts.css… Do not replace this with next/font'. D-07 (docs/DEVIATIONS.md:189-217) found that next/font served a different DM Sans build and broke the pixel gate. app/fonts.css:13-15 and app/layout.tsx:10-14 restate it. → *Use self-hosted @font-face with unicode-range (see the Cyrillic item). ## Proposed engineering queue

In order; none needs anyone outside engineering, and none changes what a respondent sees today. Each would be a D- entry and ship like the rest.

1. **Language tags at the edges (S).** A `bcp47()` helper (no → nb) for every `lang`, `hrefLang` and `inLanguage`; the survey page's `<main lang>` and the picker links still print `no`.
2. **i18n CI gates (S–M).** Parse every message with @formatjs/icu-messageformat-parser; require every CLDR plural category per locale (pl, lt and uk need one/few/many/other); compare placeholders by AST; assert GSM-7 for no/en SMS templates and normalise smart quotes on save; unit tests for plurals. Prerequisite for any new language.
3. **One locale registry (M).** lib/i18n/locales.ts as {code, bcp47, nativeName, dir, platform, survey, host, fallback}; every hard-coded list derived from it (twelve places, plus the edge function via deploy.mjs); a test that the database's check lists match.
4. **Translation governance in the database (M–L).** On app.item_translations: a status workflow (draft → in review → adjudicated → pretested → approved → retired), adjudication notes and TRAPD documents, approved rows immutable (a change is a new version), every change audited, 0079's unaudited approve functions restricted to the super-admin path, and a round's approved texts pinned when it opens. Plus variant keys for worded modules (barnehage/skole) before any is translated.
5. **Per-organisation language pilots (M).** app.locale_pilots, like module_pilots, so a language can be offered to 3–5 pilot customers (the guide's stepped rollout) before everyone.
6. **A CI job for the respondent flow (L).** The QA stack's Playwright run in CI, per locale, with a pseudo-locale (accents, 35–40 % expansion) at 320 and 390 px, axe per locale, and a contract test that no response payload carries a language.
7. **Before Ukrainian (S).** A self-hosted Cyrillic fallback font with unicode-range in app/fonts.css (never next/font, D-07).
8. **Smaller items**, each S: typed message keys (measure tsc cost first), a nynorsk-ready fallback chain in lib/i18n/request.ts when a third platform locale arrives, x-default in the sitemap or a single hreflang method, and per-host sitemaps.
